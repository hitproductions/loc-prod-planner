Loc Prod Planner — how to use it

You work in the web app. Bookmark its link — that's the tool. The Google Sheet behind it is storage; you should rarely need to open it, and there's a short list at the end of when you do.

You tell it a deadline and how many weeks of dub, edit and mix a title needs. It works out who does each phase and when, keeping everyone's calendar as clear as it can — and on the rare project where a deadline truly can't be hit any other way, it deliberately doubles someone up and tells you so, rather than missing the date.

Adding a project

In the web app: Projects → + Add project

Fill in the title, deadline, and the three phase lengths in whole weeks. Everything else has a sensible default — client is Netflix, mix level is Advanced, Music and Special are off.

The three phase lengths

Whole weeks only. Zero is valid — a mix-only job is 0/0/2. Phases run back-to-back ending the week before the deadline, so a 2/1/1 job occupies the four weeks leading up to delivery.

When you have several projects to add, add them all and plot once rather than one at a time. Each one added alone is decided before the others exist. On our test book that's the difference between three overloaded weeks and nine.

A red outline marks the specific week where someone is doubled — not the whole project. One outlined cell among five clean ones means one week is a problem.

Two views, laid out differently on purpose:

  • Projects — one row per title, weeks across. Read a project left to right.
  • Engineers — one column per person, weeks down. Everyone fits on screen, so a single row answers "who is free that week".

Set From and To to narrow it to a quarter. Clear goes back to the whole picture.

Moving a week to someone else

In Engineers view, drag a booking sideways onto another person. That week becomes theirs; the rest of the project doesn't move.

Sideways only. A row is a week, and weeks never move — the dates come from the deadline. You're moving one week of one phase, so the other weeks of that job stay put.

Before anything is written you get a summary: who it's going to, whether it double-books them, whether they're eligible. None of it stops you — a hand decision wins, same as the dropdowns on the form — but you'll know what it costs. Nothing happens until you confirm.

Changing something

Click any row in Projects to reopen it. Change what you need and Save & re-plot. The old bookings are replaced; nothing is duplicated.

Projects are listed by deadline. A–Z beside Add project sorts by title instead, which is faster when you know the name but not the date.

Retitle a project in the app, never in the sheet.

The app moves the schedule across with the new name. Editing the title cell in the sheet doesn't — the bookings keep pointing at the old name and the whole project drops off the schedule. Fixable, but see the last section.

Choosing someone yourself

Three dropdowns on the form: Recordist pick, Editor pick, Mixer pick. Leave them on Auto and the tool decides.

  • Naming a recordist alone gives them the dub and the edit — the usual case.
  • Naming an editor too splits them: recordist does the dub, editor does the edit.

A manual pick always wins, even if it double-books that person or breaks a rule. You get a warning saying exactly what it costs, and the booking is marked pinned so nothing moves it later.

Freezing a project

The Lock button on each Projects row. A locked project is left completely alone by a re-plan — use it once you've told people their dates.

Cancelling

Open the project → Cancel project. It comes off the schedule but the row stays, marked cancelled, and its history is kept.

Re-plan

In the web app: Re-plan → Preview re-plan

Over time the schedule drifts: a booking that was right when it was made isn't the best answer once the surrounding weeks fill in. Re-plan works out a better arrangement and shows you the diff before writing anything.

It will never touch:

  • Work starting this week or earlier
  • Anything you picked by hand or dragged
  • Anything you've locked

Read the change list, then Apply re-plan if it looks right. Old rows are superseded, not deleted, so nothing is lost.

What the tool decides, and what you do

The planner is deliberately narrow. It knows deadlines, phase lengths, and who is eligible for what. It does not know anything else about your people, and it is not trying to.

What it decides

  • Dates. Never negotiable — every phase is counted back from the deadline.
  • Who is eligible — can they record, edit, mix; at what level; is this a special or a music title.
  • Who gets it, balancing total workload so nobody is doubled up unless the deadline can't otherwise be hit.
  • Whether to split a job. A title stays with one person where it can. It is split when nobody is free for the whole run — better than missing the date — and the note on the project says so.

What it deliberately leaves to you

These are judgment calls. The tool has no data for them and won't pretend otherwise.

DecisionWhy it's yours
Leave, sick days, availability Not modelled at all — the tool assumes everyone is available every week. Use a manual pick or drag the week to someone else.
Sharing work out in a quiet month When there's little on, the tool won't split a job up just to give more people something. Who needs the hours, who's owed a quieter spell, who's being trained up — you know that, it doesn't. Drag a week to whoever should have it.
Who suits a client or a title Familiarity with a show, a difficult client, someone you'd rather not put on a particular job. Nothing in the data captures it.
Whether a title is harder than its week count You set the phase lengths. If a 2-week dub is really a hard 2 weeks, the tool can't tell — it counts weeks, not difficulty.
Whether a forced overlap is acceptable The tool will double someone up to hit a date and tell you it did. Whether to accept that or renegotiate the deadline is a conversation, not a calculation.

So a manual pick or a drag isn't working around the tool — it's the intended way to say "not that week, not that person." Pin it or lock it and the schedule works around you. Treat what it gives you as a strong first draft you correct.

When something looks wrong

The web app is showing old information.

Reload the page. It fetches once when it opens and doesn't notice changes made elsewhere.

A project is on the schedule but not in the Projects list.

Two causes, opposite fixes — check which before doing anything.

You renamed it in the sheet: Google Sheet → Engineer Assignment → Relink a renamed project. It moves the schedule onto the new name, same people, same weeks. Don't clear ghost projects for this — that retires the bookings you're trying to keep.

Its row was deleted by hand: Engineer Assignment → Clear ghost projects.

Someone idle wasn't given work you expected.

Google Sheet → Engineer Assignment → Why this mixer? and type the project title. It lists every engineer and why each was or wasn't eligible. The usual answer is that the tool balances total workload across all phases, so a busy recordist gets passed over for a mix.

Anything else odd.

Google Sheet → Engineer Assignment → Check bookings for problems. It reports roster mistakes, duplicated rows and bad data in one go. Worth running after anyone edits the Engineers tab.
