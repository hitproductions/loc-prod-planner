Loc Prod Planner

How to use it. Everything here takes a few seconds; nothing here can be broken permanently.

What it is

It plans localization work across the engineers — who records, who edits, who mixes, and in which week. It reads and writes the planning spreadsheet, so the sheet stays the record and you can always open it and look.

The two places

There are two, and it matters which one you are in.

  • App The planner, in a browser. Where you do the work: see the schedule, add projects, move a week, re-plan, mark things finished.
  • Sheet The spreadsheet. The record. Three things are only done here: pasting in a batch of projects, editing the engineer roster, and relinking a renamed project.

The app reads and writes the sheet, so a change in one shows up in the other. Every section below is labelled with where to be.

Reading the scheduleApp

Weeks run down the page. Use the toggle at the top left to see the columns as engineers or as projects. The row in bold is the current week. The From and To pickers narrow it to a few quarters; All puts it back.

  • Dub — recording
  • Edit
  • Mix

Outlines tell you how loaded someone is that week:

  • Blue — two jobs at once. Ordinary. A series still recording while its edit starts looks like this.
  • Red — three at once. Too much. Move one.
  • Dotted — somebody set this by hand, so the planner will not move it.

Adding one projectApp

Go to Projects and press Add. Fill in the title, client, deadline, and how many weeks each phase needs. Tick Music, Special or Atmos if they apply — they decide who is eligible. You can name a recordist, editor or mixer yourself; leave those blank to let the planner choose.

Check availability shows what would happen without saving anything. Plot & save schedules it.

Adding a project schedules only that project. Nothing already on the board moves.

Adding a batch of projectsAppSheet

Sheet Paste the rows into the Projects tab of the spreadsheet.

App Then open the app and run a Re-plan. It picks up anything that has no schedule yet.

Re-plan moves everything that is not locked — not just the rows you pasted. If you only want to add a few titles without disturbing the rest, add them one at a time through the form instead. Locking is described below.

Changing who does a weekApp

Switch the schedule to the engineers view and drag a week onto somebody else. A week can only move sideways — same week, same phase — because you are changing who does it, not when it happens. If the move creates a problem you are told before it is saved.

A cell you moved by hand shows a dotted outline. Click it to hand it back to the planner.

Re-planApp

Re-plan proposes a fresh arrangement of everything. You see what would change first, and nothing is written until you accept it.

To hold a project still, press Lock on its row in Projects — the button on the right of the list. It turns black and reads LOCKED, and the row is shaded. A locked project keeps its schedule exactly where it is and re-plan will not touch it; you can still move one of its weeks by hand. Press it again to unlock.

Completed projects lock themselves. The Locked column on the Projects tab shows the same thing, and typing Yes there still works if you prefer.

Finishing a projectApp

Open it in Projects and press Mark complete. It drops out of the working list and re-plan leaves it alone. Its bookings stay on the schedule, because the work happened and the record of who did it matters. Reopen undoes this.

Report lists what finished in a given month. Pick the month and print it.

If a project is dropped rather than finished, press Cancel project instead. That is the opposite of complete: the weeks go back to the engineers, because the work is not happening. A completed project keeps its weeks — that is the record of who did it. Both leave the working list; Show closed brings them back.

Undoing somethingApp

History lists every change, newest first, with what it did. The most recent one can be rolled back.

What the planner does not decide

It assumes everybody is available every week. It does not know who is on leave, who is coming back mid-week, or who should not be handed a difficult title right now.

That is deliberate, and it is why naming an engineer yourself is a normal thing to do rather than a workaround. When you pick someone by hand, the planner keeps your choice.

If something looks wrongAppSheet

  • App A red banner on the Projects page. Something in the engineer roster is mistyped — usually a mix level. It names the problem. Sheet Fix it in the Engineers tab of the spreadsheet.
  • App A red outline. Three jobs on one person in one week. Drag one away, or re-plan.
  • A "ghost projects" notice at the top of Projects. Something has bookings on the schedule but no row on the Projects tab, so it is holding engineer weeks that nothing can edit. There are two causes and the fixes are opposite:
    • Sheet The title was renamed. The old bookings belong to the new name. Relink them from the spreadsheet's own menu bar: Engineer Assignment → Relink a renamed project.
    • App The project is really gone. Press Clear on the notice. Its weeks go back to the engineers, and it can be undone from History.
    If you are not sure which, relink first — it is the reversible one.
  • Sheet You added an engineer and they are not in the dropdowns. The lists in the spreadsheet are built once and do not notice a new name. Run Engineer Assignment → Refresh engineer dropdowns from the spreadsheet menu.
  • App Numbers look stale. Reload the page. The app holds the book in memory for speed and re-reads it when you change something — including anything you typed straight into the spreadsheet.
